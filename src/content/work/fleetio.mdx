## Context

Fleetio is B2B SaaS for fleet maintenance and management, sold primarily through a self-serve free trial with a sales assist for larger accounts. I joined in April 2018 to own acquisition and growth and stayed through September 2025, a period in which inbound became the company's primary growth engine.

**Scope:** paid acquisition budget growing to more than $4M a year; multichannel (paid search, paid social, review sites, SEO content); a cross-functional growth team of product managers, developers, designers and engineers.

## The problem

When I arrived, paid spend was optimized to top-of-funnel events. The ad platforms were doing exactly what they were told: finding cheap signups. The signups weren't reliably the accounts that became revenue, and there weren't yet enough closed-won events per week to bid on revenue directly.

The trial itself was the second leak. Plenty of good-fit accounts started a trial and never reached the moment where the product proved its value.

## What I did

**1. Bid on value, not volume.** I put in place the [yield and bounty framework](/writing/yields-and-bounties): assign each funnel event a dollar *bounty* derived from its downstream conversion rate, report *yield* (bounty value ÷ spend) by campaign, and step the optimization event down the funnel as volume allowed. This let us scale spend without the usual drift toward junk leads.

**2. Find the winning segments inside the averages.** Blended conversion rates hid large differences by segment. We broke trial-to-paid down by firmographics and intent signals, pushed the best-converting segments back to the ad platforms as conversion values and lookalike seeds, and excluded the worst. (The general method is in [Metrics in Metrics](/writing/metrics-in-metrics).)

**3. SEO content for high-intent segments.** Rather than chasing volume keywords, we built content around the specific jobs and problems of the segments that converted best, so organic compounded alongside paid instead of competing with it.

**4. Make activation a growth problem.** I hired and led a cross-functional growth team, with product, engineering and design inside the growth org, to ideate, prototype and ship onboarding and lifecycle experiments. We sized tests honestly ([big swings for small samples](/writing/how-to-run-a-proper-ab-test)) and kept what moved trial-to-paid.

## Results

- **10x growth in inbound ARR** over the tenure.
- **Trial-to-paid conversion doubled**, improving activation and long-term customer value.
- A paid program that scaled past **$4M a year** while staying accountable to revenue, not leads.

## What I’d do differently

Pull product and growth together even earlier. The biggest single gains came once engineers sat inside the growth team, and every quarter before that was a quarter of experiments waiting in someone else's backlog.
